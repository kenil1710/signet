How exposed is your dApp's front end?

Enter a domain. We load its page once, from our server, and report what a front-end attacker could use: scripts from other origins without SRI, missing security headers, mixed content, the registrar lock and DNS hints, and whether its releases are sealed.

What it checks

  • Scripts and styles from other origins in the HTML, and whether each has an SRI integrity hash. Without one, whoever controls that origin (a CDN, a widget vendor) can change your code.
  • Security headers: Content-Security-Policy, Strict-Transport-Security, X-Content-Type-Options, framing.
  • Mixed content: anything loaded over plain http://.
  • Domain: the registrar's transfer lock (RDAP), name servers, CAA and DNSSEC (public DNS).
  • Releases: whether the domain is sealed with Signet (/.well-known/signet.json).

What it can't see

  • Scripts added at runtime and chunks loaded later: the page's JavaScript is not run.
  • What the site asks a wallet to sign.
  • Whether today's files are the ones your team reviewed. That is what sealing your releases proves.
  • Your hosting, CDN and registrar account security (2FA, API keys).

Privacy: the check runs on our server, so your browser never contacts the checked site. We keep no record of who asked. Each domain's report is held in Cloudflare's cache for up to 10 minutes, so repeated checks don't load the site again, and is then dropped; nothing else is kept. To limit abuse, your IP address is held in memory for one minute (10 checks a minute per address).
